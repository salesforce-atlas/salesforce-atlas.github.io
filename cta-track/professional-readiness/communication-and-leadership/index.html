    Communication & Leadership for Architects¶

    Essential Soft Skills for CTA Success¶

    While technical skills get you to the CTA review board, communication and leadership skills determine your success. This guide focuses on developing these critical competencies.

    Executive Communication¶

    Speaking the Language of Business¶

    Avoid:¶

    "We need to implement Platform Events with a publish-subscribe pattern using streaming API to decouple our monolithic architecture..."

    Instead:¶

    "This approach will reduce system downtime by 50% and allow us to add new features 3x faster, improving time-to-market and customer satisfaction."

    The Business Translation Framework¶

    1
    Technical Feature → Business Capability → Business Value → Financial Impact

    Example: 1. Technical: Platform Events for async processing 2. Business: Real-time order processing without system blocking 3. Value: Faster order fulfillment, better customer experience 4. Financial: 20% increase in order throughput = $2M additional annual revenue

    Executive Presentation Structure¶

    The Pyramid Principle¶

    1
2
3
4
5
6
7
    Start with conclusion
    ↓
Support with 3 key points
    ↓
Provide evidence for each
    ↓
End with call-to-action

    Example: - Conclusion: "We should implement Salesforce CPQ" - Key Points: 1. Reduces quote time from 2 days to 2 hours 2. Eliminates 95% of pricing errors 3. Provides real-time visibility into pipeline

    • Evidence: ROI calculation, customer case studies, pilot results
    • Action: "Approve $200K budget to start in Q3"

    Whiteboarding Mastery¶

    The 15-Minute Architecture Presentation¶

    Minute 1-2: Context & Requirements - Business problem - Key stakeholders - Success criteria

    Minute 3-8: Solution Architecture - High-level architecture diagram - Data model sketch - Integration points - Security model

    Minute 9-12: Key Design Decisions - Critical choices made - Trade-offs considered - Risks and mitigations

    Minute 13-15: Summary & Next Steps - Recap solution - Highlight business value - Open for questions

    Whiteboarding Techniques¶

    Progressive Disclosure¶

    Don't draw everything at once: 1. Start with high-level boxes 2. Add connections 3. Drill into one area 4. Add security/details 5. Tie back to business value

    Visual Hierarchy¶

    1
2
3
4
    Most Important → Largest, centered
Supporting Details → Smaller, around edges
Flows → Arrows with labels
Annotations → Different color or style

    Common Whiteboard Mistakes¶

    ❌ Drawing first, explaining later ✅ Explain as you draw

    ❌ Messy, illegible writing ✅ Practice writing clearly and large

    ❌ Blocking the view ✅ Stand to the side, point with open hand

    ❌ Too much detail too early ✅ Start high-level, add detail as needed

    Leadership Scenarios¶

    Scenario 1: Disagreeing Stakeholders¶

    Situation: Sales VP wants custom solution, CIO wants standard CPQ.

    Your Role: Find common ground and build consensus.

    Approach: 1. Listen to both: Understand underlying concerns

    • Sales: "We're unique, need flexibility"

    • CIO: "Need supportable, maintainable solution"

    • Find common ground: Both want fast time-to-market and low risk

    • Propose hybrid: CPQ standard features + targeted customization

    • Address 80% needs with CPQ

    • Custom development for truly unique 20%

    • Phased approach to prove value

    • Build consensus: Workshop to map requirements to CPQ features

    Outcome: Agreement on CPQ with limited customization

    Scenario 2: Technical Team Resistance¶

    Situation: Development team resists your architecture recommendation.

    Your Role: Influence without direct authority.

    Approach: 1. Listen to concerns: "What specifically concerns you?"

    • Concern: "This is more complex than our current approach"

    • Acknowledge validity: "You're right, it is more complex initially"

    • Explain rationale: "But it solves the scalability issue that's causing production incidents"

    • Involve in solution: "How can we make this easier to implement?"

    • Compromise where possible: Accept their suggestion for phased rollout

    Outcome: Team buy-in with modified implementation plan

    Scenario 3: Executive Escalation¶

    Situation: Project at risk due to scope creep. Executive sponsor uninformed.

    Your Role: Escalate appropriately and manage expectations.

    Approach: 1. Prepare facts:

    • Original scope: 100 stories
    • Current scope: 150 stories (+50%)

    • Impact: 3-month delay OR reduce scope

    • Frame the conversation:

    • "I need your help with a decision..."

    • Not: "We have a problem..."

    • Present options:

    • Option A: Delay launch, deliver full scope

    • Option B: Launch on time, phase 2 for additional features

    • Option C: Add resources (cost increase)

    • Recommend: "I recommend Option B because..."

    • Get decision: Clear owner and timeline

    Outcome: Executive decides on Option B, communicates to organization

    Influence Techniques¶

    Cialdini's 6 Principles¶

    1. Reciprocity: Give value first

    2. Share knowledge freely

    3. Help others solve problems

    4. Build goodwill

    5. Commitment: Start small

    6. Get agreement on principles

    7. Build on small wins

    8. Public commitments stick

    9. Social Proof: Show others doing it

    10. "Salesforce recommends..."

    11. "Industry leaders use..."

    12. Customer success stories

    13. Authority: Establish credibility

    14. Certifications and expertise

    15. Past successes

    16. Data and research

    17. Liking: Build relationships

    18. Find common ground

    19. Show genuine interest

    20. Be personable

    21. Scarcity: Create urgency

    22. "Limited-time pricing"

    23. "Resource availability"
    24. "Competitive pressure"

    Difficult Conversations¶

    The DESC Method¶

    Describe the situation objectively Express your concerns Suggest alternatives Consequences (positive and negative)

    Example: - Describe: "The data migration is 2 weeks behind schedule" - Express: "I'm concerned we'll miss the go-live date" - Suggest: "Can we bring in additional resources or reduce initial data scope?" - Consequences: "This keeps us on track (positive). If we don't act now, we risk 4-week delay impacting revenue (negative)"

    Handling Conflict¶

    Five Conflict Styles¶

    1. Avoiding: Ignore the conflict

    2. When: Low stakes, need time to cool down

    3. Risk: Conflict festers

    4. Accommodating: Give in to others

    5. When: Preserve relationship, low importance to you

    6. Risk: Your needs unmet

    7. Competing: Assert your position

    8. When: Emergency, critical issue, you have expertise

    9. Risk: Damage relationships

    10. Compromising: Meet in the middle

    11. When: Time pressure, equal power

    12. Risk: Partial solution, neither side fully satisfied

    13. Collaborating: Win-win solution

    14. When: Time available, relationship important, creative solution possible

    15. Best outcome but takes effort

    For CTA: Default to Collaborating, use others situationally

    Meeting Leadership¶

    Running Effective Architecture Reviews¶

    Before the Meeting¶

    • [ ] Clear agenda sent 24 hours ahead
    • [ ] Pre-read materials distributed
    • [ ] Right people invited (decision-makers + experts)
    • [ ] Time-boxed agenda items

    During the Meeting¶

    • [ ] Start on time
    • [ ] Restate objective and desired outcome
    • [ ] Facilitate, don't dominate
    • [ ] Park off-topic items
    • [ ] Drive to decisions
    • [ ] End with clear action items (owner, due date)
    • [ ] End on time

    After the Meeting¶

    • [ ] Send notes within 24 hours
    • [ ] Follow up on action items
    • [ ] Update decision log

    Facilitation Techniques¶

    Encourage Participation: - "What are your thoughts, [Name]?" - Round-robin for input - Anonymous voting for controversial decisions

    Manage Dominators: - "Thanks [Name], let's hear from others" - Set speaking time limits - Offline follow-up for deep dives

    Drive to Decisions: - "It sounds like we're aligned on..." - "Let's decide: Option A or B?" - "Who will make the final call?"

    Practice Exercises¶

    Exercise 1: Elevator Pitch¶

    Explain your last project's architecture in 60 seconds to:

    • Your CEO
    • A developer
    • A non-technical stakeholder

    Exercise 2: Objection Handling¶

    Practice responding to:

    • "This is too expensive"
    • "We don't have time"
    • "This is too complex"
    • "Why not use [alternative]?"

    Exercise 3: Stakeholder Mapping¶

    For your current project:

    • List all stakeholders
    • Rate power/interest
    • Plan engagement strategy
    • Document concerns
