    Scenario Defense Cases¶

    Practicing Technical Defense¶

    This guide provides practice scenarios specifically focused on defending your technical decisions during the CTA review board Q&A phase.

    Scenario Defense Framework¶

    When defending a technical decision, use this structure:

    1. Context: Restate the requirement/constraint
    2. Decision: What you chose
    3. Rationale: Why you chose it
    4. Trade-offs: What you gave up
    5. Alternatives: What else you considered

    Defense Case 1: Large Data Volume Handling¶

    Scenario: Customer has 50 million records in Account object with complex reporting needs.

    Your Decision: Implement Big Objects for historical data + data archiving strategy

    Panel Challenge: "Why not just keep everything in standard objects and use indexes?"

    Defense Response:

    "That's a great question. While indexes do improve query performance, there are several factors that led me to recommend Big Objects:

    Context: With 50M records, we're approaching storage limits and facing performance degradation even with selective indexes.

    Decision: I proposed a tiered data strategy - keep 2 years of active data in standard objects, archive older data to Big Objects.

    Rationale: - Storage costs: Big Objects don't count against standard storage limits - Query performance: Users primarily need recent data (80/20 rule) - Reporting: Can still query Big Objects via SOQL for historical analysis - Governor limits: Reduces risk of hitting query row limits

    Trade-offs: - Big Objects have limitations (no standard UI, async writes only) - Need to build custom UI for historical data access - Additional development effort for archiving automation

    Alternatives Considered: - External storage (Heroku Postgres): More expensive, integration overhead - Standard objects with aggressive indexing: Hits storage limits, degraded performance - Data warehouse (Tableau CRM): Great for analytics but doesn't solve transactional storage issue

    Given the 50M record volume and growth trajectory, the Big Objects approach provides the best balance of cost, performance, and scalability."

    Key Learning: Show you've thought through multiple options and can justify your choice with data.

    Defense Case 2: Integration Pattern Selection¶

    Scenario: Need to integrate Salesforce with 5 external systems (ERP, Marketing, Support, HR, Analytics).

    Your Decision: Implement MuleSoft as integration hub using API-led connectivity.

    Panel Challenge: "MuleSoft is expensive. Why not use point-to-point REST APIs?"

    Defense Response:

    "Excellent point about cost. Let me walk through my thinking:

    Context: We have 5 systems today, likely to grow to 8-10 over next 2 years based on business roadmap.

    Cost Analysis: - Point-to-point: 5 systems = 10 integrations (n*(n-1)/2) - With MuleSoft hub: 5 integrations (n) - As we grow to 10 systems: 45 vs 10 integrations

    Decision: MuleSoft API-led connectivity

    Rationale: - Scalability: Linear growth vs. exponential - Reusability: Process and Experience APIs can be reused - Maintenance: Centralized transformation logic - Monitoring: Single pane of glass for all integrations - Enterprise features: Rate limiting, caching, security

    Trade-offs: - Higher upfront cost and licensing - Learning curve for MuleSoft platform - Additional infrastructure to manage

    Alternatives: - Salesforce Platform Events: Great for Salesforce-centric flows, limited for external orchestration - Custom middleware on Heroku: Lower cost but need to build/maintain - Point-to-point: Simple initially, becomes unmaintainable at scale

    ROI Calculation: - Year 1: Higher cost (MuleSoft licensing + implementation) - Year 2-3: Break-even (reduced integration maintenance) - Year 4+: Significant savings (faster time-to-market for new integrations)

    Given the integration roadmap and total cost of ownership over 3-5 years, MuleSoft provides better long-term value despite higher initial investment."

    Key Learning: Defend with business case, not just technical features.

    Defense Case 3: Security Model Design¶

    Scenario: Global sales organization with complex data visibility requirements.

    Your Decision: Private OWD + sharing rules + territory management.

    Panel Challenge: "Why not just use Public Read/Write and restrict with permission sets?"

    Defense Response:

    "That's an interesting approach. Let me explain why I chose a restrictive model:

    Security Principle: Least privilege - start restrictive, open up as needed vs. start open, try to restrict.

    Decision: Private OWD with explicit sharing

    Rationale: - Compliance: GDPR requires data minimization and access controls - Audit trail: Easier to explain why someone HAS access vs. why they DON'T - Scalability: Adding access via sharing is safer than removing via permission sets - Territory changes: Sharing automatically updates when territories change

    Trade-offs: - More complex initial setup - Need to carefully design sharing rules - Performance consideration with many sharing rules (though minimal impact with proper design)

    Risk Analysis: - Public Read/Write risk: Accidental data exposure, compliance violation, difficult to audit - Private with sharing risk: User can't see data they need (mitigated by testing and UAT)

    Alternatives: - Public Read Only + restrict via permission sets: Doesn't solve write access control - Record types + page layouts: UI-level security only, not true data security - Public Read/Write + field-level security: Still allows record visibility

    In a regulated industry with global data privacy requirements, the risk of over-exposure with Public OWD outweighs the complexity of implementing proper sharing rules."

    Key Learning: Emphasize risk management and compliance, not just technical implementation.

    Defense Case 4: Scalability Decision¶

    Scenario: High-volume transaction processing with 100,000 records/hour during peak.

    Your Decision: Queueable Apex + Platform Events + batch processing hybrid approach.

    Panel Challenge: "Why not use @future methods? They're simpler."

    Defense Response:

    "Great question. @future methods are simpler, but they have limitations that make them unsuitable for this use case:

    Requirement Analysis: - Volume: 100K records/hour = ~28 records/second - Complexity: Multi-step processing with dependencies - Monitoring: Need visibility into processing status - Error handling: Must retry failed records

    @future Limitations: - No chaining (can't call another @future from @future) - No monitoring of individual jobs - Limited to 250 concurrent jobs - No return values or status tracking

    My Approach: 1. Platform Events for ingest (high-volume, non-blocking) 2. Event trigger → Queueable Apex for processing 3. Queueable chains for multi-step workflows 4. Batch Apex for cleanup/reconciliation

    Why This Works: - Platform Events decouple ingest from processing - Queueable allows chaining and monitoring - Can return IDs for status tracking - Better governor limit management (50 concurrent Queueables vs 250 @future limit sounds better, but we can chain)

    Trade-offs: - More complex architecture - Need to manage event replay for failures - Additional platform event license consumption

    Alternatives: - Batch Apex only: Too slow for real-time requirements (runs every 5 minutes minimum) - @future only: Hits limitations with chaining and monitoring - External processing (Heroku): Adds infrastructure and cost

    The hybrid approach provides the right balance of real-time processing, scalability, and observability."

    Key Learning: Demonstrate deep understanding of platform capabilities and limitations.

    Defense Case 5: Custom vs. Standard Solution¶

    Scenario: Sales team wants custom quoting tool instead of Salesforce CPQ.

    Your Decision: Recommend Salesforce CPQ despite custom request.

    Panel Challenge: "The client explicitly asked for custom. Why are you pushing back?"

    Defense Response:

    "This is where my role as a trusted advisor comes in. Let me explain my recommendation:

    Client Request: Custom quoting tool (estimated $500K custom development)

    My Recommendation: Salesforce CPQ (estimated $200K implementation)

    Analysis of Requirements: - Product bundles and configurations - Volume-based discounting - Approval workflows - Integration with ERP for invoicing - Quote document generation

    Why CPQ Fits: - 80% out-of-box: Core requirements are standard CPQ features - Proven: Battle-tested in thousands of implementations - Supported: Salesforce maintains and enhances - Scalable: Built for enterprise-scale quoting - Time to value: 3-4 months vs 9-12 months custom

    Client Concerns (addressed): 1. "We're unique": Every company thinks they're unique. After analysis, 80% of requirements are standard 2. "Need flexibility": CPQ is highly configurable without custom code 3. "Expensive": Initial license cost higher, but TCO lower (no custom maintenance)

    Custom Development Risks: - Maintenance burden: Custom code requires ongoing support - Upgrade challenges: Breaks with platform upgrades - Knowledge dependency: Risk if custom developer leaves - Feature gap: Need to build features CPQ includes (guided selling, renewal management, etc.)

    My Approach: 1. Run 2-week discovery workshop 2. Map requirements to CPQ features 3. Identify true customization needs (likely 20%) 4. Show CPQ demo with their data 5. Build business case: TCO comparison

    Outcome: - If truly unique: Document specific gaps and build custom - If standard: Implement CPQ and save $300K + reduce risk

    As a CTA, I owe the client my honest assessment, not just agreement with their initial request. This is about long-term success, not short-term approval."

    Key Learning: Show strategic thinking and ability to challenge assumptions respectfully.

    Practice Method¶

    Solo Practice¶

    1. Read scenario
    2. Make a decision
    3. Write your defense (5-minute timer)
    4. Record yourself presenting
    5. Review and refine

    Group Practice¶

    1. Present your scenario to study group
    2. Have group play "panel members"
    3. Field challenging questions
    4. Get feedback on:
    5. Clarity of explanation
    6. Confidence in delivery
    7. Handling of objections
    8. Technical accuracy

    Continuous Improvement¶

    • Keep a decision log for each practice scenario
    • Note questions you struggled with
    • Research areas of weakness
    • Re-practice difficult scenarios

    Common Defense Mistakes to Avoid¶

    ❌ Getting defensive or argumentative ✅ Stay calm, acknowledge the point, explain reasoning

    ❌ Making up answers you don't know ✅ "I don't know that specific detail, but here's how I'd find out..."

    ❌ Only giving one option ✅ "I considered X, Y, and Z. Here's why I chose X..."

    ❌ Ignoring business context ✅ Connect technical decisions to business value

    ❌ Over-complicating explanations ✅ Clear, concise responses with appropriate detail

    Practice these scenarios until defense becomes second nature!
